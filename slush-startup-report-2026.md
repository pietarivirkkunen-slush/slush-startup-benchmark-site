# Slush Startup Report 2026 — Full Summary

**A matched benchmark of 2,108 pre-Series B European startups against 229,866 peers.**

> This document contains the complete findings, numbers, and methodology of the Slush Startup Report 2026. It is designed to be read by a human or given to an LLM for further analysis or Q&A.

---

## What this study is

Slush is Europe's largest startup event, held each November in Helsinki. This study asks: do the startups that attend Slush go on to outperform comparable peers?

To answer it, we tracked **2,108 European companies** that attended Slush for the first time in 2022, 2023, or 2024 — all pre-Series B at attendance, founded 2016 or later — and compared their post-attendance outcomes against **229,866 matched European peers** from the same stage, country, and founding vintage. The observation window opens the day after each company's first attendance.

The 2,108 companies come from a paid-ticket universe of 4,501 unique companies across Europe and the US over 2022–2025 (de-duplicated to first attendance). Europe-only, 2022–2024 cohorts, founded 2016+, pre-Series B filters reduce that to the analysis population.

**The study is observational, not causal.** Matching controls for founding year, geography, and stage. It cannot control for the ambition or fundraising-readiness of founders who choose to attend. The premium reflects a combination of who attends and what attending does; the data cannot separate the two.

## Finding 1: The fundraising premium

**Slush companies raised institutional capital at 3.5× the rate of comparable European peers.** The premium holds in all three cohorts and every European region tested.

Among the 48% of attendees who arrived without prior backing, the post-event VC rate is 3.1× that of comparable peers — making Slush a measurable catalyst on the path to first institutional capital.

### Cohort breakdown

| Cohort | Slush rate | Benchmark rate | Premium | Observation window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 32.80% | 7.20% | 4.6× | 3 years post-event |
| 2023 | 26.80% | 4.90% | 5.5× | 2 years post-event |
| 2024 | 17.90% | 2.80% | 6.4× | 1 year post-event |
| All cohorts combined | 26.40% | 7.50% | 3.5× | — |

Note: Newer cohorts show higher apparent premiums partly because their benchmark comparison window is shorter — the benchmark pool anchored to Nov 2022 shows lower cumulative rates over shorter windows.

### By starting stage

| Stage at first attendance | Slush n | Slush rate | Benchmark rate | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bootstrapped (no prior VC) | 1,009 (48%) | 17.00% | 5.60% | 3.1× |
| Already VC-backed | 1,099 (52%) | 35.00% | 23.20% | 1.5× |

In the European benchmark, 89% of companies are bootstrapped — making the Slush population unusually skewed toward already-backed companies. The aggregate 3.5× is higher than both subgroup multiples because of this composition effect (Simpson's paradox): backed companies have higher absolute rates in both populations, and Slush over-represents them.

### Median round size

Among funded companies only: Slush median $3.8M vs benchmark $1.8M (2.1×). Based on n=557 Slush companies and 17,163 benchmark companies with at least one qualifying post-event VC round.

**VC round types counted:** Seed, Angel, Early VC, Series A–H, Growth Equity VC, Late VC, Convertible, Private Placement VC. Grants and debt excluded.

## Finding 2: The cap table premium

**Slush companies are 7.0× more likely than comparable European peers to be backed by a top-0.1% global VC** (ranked by Dealroom's global investor index). The overall fundraising premium is 3.5×. The fund-quality premium is twice that.

The ratio rises from 5.1× at the top decile to 7.0× at the top 0.1% — the Slush cohort is concentrated toward the top of the VC quality spectrum.

### Investor tier definitions (Dealroom global rank)

Dealroom assigns a global rank to approximately 40,000 investors based on deal activity, portfolio quality, and exits. For each company, the analysis uses the single best investor rank across all qualifying post-attendance rounds.

| Tier | Rank threshold | Slush rate | Benchmark rate | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Top 0.1% | ≤ 40 | 5.60% | 0.80% | 7.0× |
| Top 1% | ≤ 400 | 17.30% | 2.80% | 6.1× |
| Top 2.5% | ≤ 1,000 | 24.30% | 4.20% | 5.8× |
| Top 10% | ≤ 4,000 | 36.00% | 7.10% | 5.1× |

~24.5% of Dealroom-tracked investors hold a power-law rank. Companies whose cap tables contain only unranked investors are excluded from tier comparisons.

### Post-event investor acquisition (Slush companies only, n=2,108)

| Metric | Rate |
|---|---|
| Any new investor post-event | 28.90% |
| New top-10% global VC | 12.20% |
| New top-1% global VC | 4.40% |
| New US investor | 7.00% |
| New international (non-European) VC | 10.30% |

### Cross-border investor reach

Slush also attracted roughly 135 US-domiciled companies over the study period — excluded from the main 2,108 analysis but tracked separately.

| Direction | Slush rate | Benchmark rate | Premium | Slush n | Benchmark n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| European company + US investor | 22.20% | 4.50% | 5× | 469 | 10,177 |
| US company + European investor | 37.00% | 4.60% | 8× | 50 | 8,580 |

Note: The US→EU benchmark uses Dealroom's global US company dataset (~185,800 companies), not the 229,866 European benchmark used elsewhere.

## Finding 3: Growth velocity

### Stage graduation

**Slush companies advance at least one funding stage at 2.5× the rate of peers.** 13.7% of the cohort moved up at least one stage vs 5.4% of peers.

The graduation premium is widest at the bootstrapped layer. Using the 2022 cohort (three full years of post-event data, the most mature reference):

| Stage at attendance | Slush 2022 rate | Benchmark rate | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bootstrapped → first VC round | 20.6% | 5.6% | 3.7× |
| Seed / Angel → Early VC | 23.0% | 11.9% | 1.9× |
| Early VC / Series A → Growth | 10.3% | 5.5% | 1.9× |

The 2023 and 2024 cohorts show higher apparent premiums (4.2× and 5.7× for the bootstrapped layer) because their shorter observation windows produce lower absolute benchmark rates. These are expected to converge as data matures.

### Headcount growth

61.1% of Slush companies with measurable headcount data grew their teams post-event, vs 49.4% of peers — a 1.2× premium. Dealroom employee data is available for roughly 70% of companies; only the ~43% with non-zero records at both the start and end of the observation window are included (n=910 Slush, 30,952 benchmark). This is a directional signal, not a precise measure.

### Grant / non-dilutive funding

23.8% of Slush companies received a grant or support-programme allocation post-event, vs 7.1% of peers — a 3.4× premium. Tracked separately from VC to avoid conflating dilutive and non-dilutive capital. For deep tech, climate, and research-intensive companies, non-dilutive funding typically runs alongside equity, not instead of it.

## Methodology

### Matching

The benchmark of 229,866 Dealroom-tracked European companies is matched to the Slush population on founding year, country, and growth stage. For Slush companies, the observation window opens the day after first attendance. For benchmark peers, it covers the equivalent post-2022 period.

### Match quality (from Slush ticket data to Dealroom)

| Method | n | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Exact company name | 2,904 | 64.5% |
| Website domain | 478 | 10.6% |
| Dealroom URL slug | 37 | 0.8% |
| Fuzzy name (confirmed) | 26 | 0.6% |
| No match (excluded) | 1,044 | 23.2% |

Overall match rate: 76.8%

### Population exclusion log

| Filter step | Remaining n |
|---|---|
| Paid-ticket universe (de-duplicated) | 4,501 |
| After Dealroom match | 3,457 |
| Europe-only filter | ~3,320 |
| Founding year ≥ 2016 | ~2,980 |
| Pre-Series B at first attendance | ~2,780 |
| 2022–2024 cohorts only (exclude 2025) | **2,108** |

### Why 2025 is excluded

Six months of post-event data is not enough to register the rounds that will eventually appear; the measured rate is artificially low. The 2025 cohort enters the analysis in the 2027 edition.

### Benchmark time anchor caveat

For Slush companies, quarters are measured from November 1st of the attendance year. The benchmark is a single pool anchored to November 1, 2022 — every benchmark company's Q1–Q12 starts there. A 2023 or 2024 Slush cohort row and the benchmark row therefore cover different calendar windows. The design measures relative pace, not performance within a shared calendar period.

### Caveats

- **Observational, not causal.** The premium reflects a combination of selection and treatment effects that cannot be separated from this data.
- **Dealroom coverage is stronger in Western Europe.** This dampens both Slush and benchmark figures in Central and Eastern Europe.
- **Investor enrichment data is partial.** AUM covers 6.5% of investors; power-law rank covers ~24.5% of tracked investors.
- **Survivorship bias applies to both sides.** Dealroom under-represents failed companies. This may inflate absolute rates on both sides but should not materially affect ratios.
- **Headcount data is directional.** Only ~43% of companies have non-zero employee records at both the start and end of the observation window.
